STAGE 03 // FULL_SCREEN_NEURAL_NET · EXPERIENCE

Engineering & Research Log

Click any node to expand verified telemetry

  • Tools.AI runs search over a catalog of AI tools. I built the yardstick that tells the team whether a change to search actually makes results better, with error bars instead of gut feel.

  • Built a search-quality benchmark (25 queries with 19 held out, 6 multi-step workflows, ~1,990 graded relevance pairs) and a one-command Python harness that scores any search setup on recall@5/10, nDCG@10 and MRR with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals.
  • Compared 55 search configurations (4 embedding models, 9 listing-text versions, BM25 hybrid) and recommended a candidate scoring about 12% higher nDCG@10 than the current model on held-out queries. The CEO independently reproduced all 55 runs.
  • Extended the benchmark to score multi-step workflow answers, showing that similarity scores can’t separate “no tool fits” from a weak match.
  • Traced latency and run-to-run variance to the embedding hosting providers and proposed a one-line fix (pin one provider).

  • Sequence alignment lines up two DNA or protein sequences to find where they match. Doing it exactly normally needs memory that grows with the product of both lengths, which is impossible for long reads. TILE gets the exact answer in a tiny fraction of that memory.

  • Design and build TILE, an exact sequence aligner in C++ with AVX2 SIMD that stores only tile borders and recomputes just the tiles on the optimal path, cutting traceback memory from O(mn) to O(mn/T + T²); border cells compressed from 5 bytes to 2.
  • Vectorized the inner loop (32 cells per instruction, up to 3× on proteins) and multithreaded tiles (7× on 8 threads), aligning real 500k-base Nanopore read pairs exactly in 4.4 s under 0.5 GB, where a full matrix needs 250+ GB.
  • Benchmarked against Myers–Miller, BiWFA, ksw2, parasail and TALCO: 5–26× faster than Myers–Miller on DNA and up to 23× under TWILIGHT-like scoring, optimal on every pair; fuzz-tested.
  • Porting TILE to CUDA for integration into TWILIGHT, the lab’s published GPU-accelerated multiple sequence alignment tool.

  • Onye builds AI tools for healthcare. I work on making sure clinical data is valid and that the LLM answers built on it are measured before anyone relies on them.

  • Build pipelines validating HL7/FHIR R4 schemas (DiagnosticReport, Patient) and pytest/Pydantic mocks that stress-test EHR APIs.
  • Develop evaluation harnesses benchmarking clinical LLM answers on hallucination rate, latency, and reference similarity.
  • Evaluate embedding retrieval recall on simulated patient-record queries, documenting edge cases and reliability findings.

  • Wrote research-grade math and science problems hard enough to challenge frontier AI models, each with a solver that computes the exact answer and a second, independent solver that checks it.

  • Built 11 complete problem packages in about 10 days, each with 4 linked computational steps, a reference solver, an independently written cross-check solver, and 48–104 test cases.
  • 2 problems accepted after expert review; a third passed every automated stage and was in expert review when the project closed.
  • Bifurcation census of chemical reaction networks (accepted): counts steady states and locates saddle-node and Hopf bifurcations with fully exact computer algebra (Gröbner bases, FGLM, exact real-root isolation), resolving answers from 1e-32 to 1e14 that floating point misclassifies.
  • Escape energies at degenerate equilibria (accepted): exact series expansions where linearization fails, built to catch near-ties as small as 1 − 2−1700.
  • Long-time asymptotics of linear systems: finds hidden rational relations among frequencies with LLL lattice reduction, then maximizes over the resulting torus with certified branch-and-bound.
  • Also built: eigenvalue limits of banded block-Toeplitz matrices (verified by high-precision LU to n = 800), shape-dependent lattice sums, Metzler-matrix thresholds, and stochastic enzyme first-passage statistics.
  • Exact or certified arithmetic everywhere, never ad hoc tolerances. Wrote a checker that maps every sentence of a problem spec to the tests that verify it.

  • My first research lab, before college. A cell’s stiffness is a physical signature of its health, and we measured it without dyes or labels.

  • Used quantitative phase imaging (QPI), a label-free microscopy method that measures how much a cell delays light passing through it, to study elasticity and mechanical behavior across mammalian cell lines.
  • Cell types studied included U87 glioblastoma (brain cancer) cells and red blood cells.
